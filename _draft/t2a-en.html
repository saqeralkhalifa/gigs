About the book

The ballot was never the destination. It was the detour.

For a century we have been told that democracy is the end of history, the final, unimprovable form of government. Shackles of Democracy argues the opposite: the machinery of mass elections is failing on its own terms, and this century will finish the job.

Drawing on political history, institutional decay, and the technologies now rewriting how consent is manufactured, the book shows how the vote became a ritual that constrains citizens more than it empowers them, and why the systems replacing it are already taking shape.

Confrontational, rigorous, and unafraid of its own conclusions, it does not mourn the ballot. It asks the harder question: what should come after?

“The decline is already measurable.”

Not a prediction, a trend line. Built from Freedom House, V-Dem, the World Bank, and the sources named in the text.

“The century rewards almost everything democracy is worst at.”

Speed, secrecy, and long horizons, and it punishes almost everything democracy was built to do.

“A competition for power wearing the costume of public service.”

How elections stopped selecting for governing and started selecting for winning.

The second half designs what should come after. And the final chapter tells you exactly how to prove the whole argument wrong.
